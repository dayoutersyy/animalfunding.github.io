Campus Animal Rescue Story

388 RMB: A Beginning

A restrained record of how student volunteers turned two years of routine shelter work into a shared public action.

What can 388 RMB do?

Before the First Invitation

Before last week, Brandon did not have a clear answer either. Then he stood at the entrance of the school cafeteria, faced passing classmates, took a breath, and said his first invitation out loud.

The 388 RMB was the first direct response after two years of volunteer shifts at a stray animal shelter. Not large in amount, but heavy in meaning.

2024: Entering the Shelter

In 2024, Brandon, then in junior high, came across a post about a stray cat and dog shelter in Chongqing. The shelter had been running for more than forty years, and Grandma Chen had put almost all she had into caring for abandoned animals.

Brandon became a volunteer and kept going once or twice each month, except during the busiest exam period. A normal shift lasted about three hours: cleaning litter, refilling water, and doing basic sanitation for hundreds of cats.

A Turning Point in Mid-April

In mid-April this year, he learned that an auntie who took care of cats at the shelter became ill and could no longer continue the work. That moment changed his understanding of responsibility.

He decided to take on more, and to invite more people into the work.

The People Behind the Project

Lilith, who had also volunteered since 2024, Thomas, who cared about animals and documented activities through photography, and Dustin, who joined after seeing Brandon's post, became part of the team.

What started with one student became a group of four, and later a broader collaboration of around ten students, including poster design and website support.

From Recording to Public Sharing

The team documented a real volunteer session without staged shots, designed posters centered on individual cats and their real backgrounds, and ran a campus sharing event with an opening, documentary screening, story sharing, and a donation explanation.

During the donation moment, participation stayed voluntary. Before any formal donation channel was confirmed, students and teachers who wanted to support the action contributed 388 RMB in advance.

The amount was small, but the team treated it seriously, with plans to publish follow-up process information and clear records.
